# Inclusion

This page covers the rules on the school inclusion of students with disabilities and with specific learning disorders
(DSA), for the part that concerns the library. It is written for the head teacher, for the inclusion officers and for
whoever at the school is evaluating BiblioViva. Those rules set no requirements for library management software: they
ask the school to give the students the tools and materials they need. BiblioViva therefore has a limited role, which
the page describes.

## What the rule asks

- Law of 8 October 2010, no. 170 (Legge 170/2010), on specific learning disorders (dyslexia, dysgraphia, dysorthography,
  dyscalculia). Students diagnosed with a DSA have the right to dispensatory and compensatory measures of teaching
  flexibility (art. 5, paragraph 1). Schools guarantee individualised and personalised teaching and the introduction of
  compensatory tools, including alternative learning aids and information technology (art. 5, paragraph 2, points a
  and b).
- Guidelines attached to the ministerial decree of 12 July 2011, no. 5669 (D.M. 5669/2011). They implement Law 170/2010
  and say how the school organises the tools and measures for students with a DSA, starting from the personalised
  learning plan.
- Law of 5 February 1992, no. 104 (Legge 104/1992), on assistance, social integration and the rights of persons with
  disabilities. School integration is also achieved by providing schools with technical equipment, teaching aids and
  every form of technical aid (art. 13, paragraph 1, point b), and with support activities (art. 13, paragraph 3).
- The Inclusion Plan (legislative decree of 13 April 2017, no. 66, D.Lgs. 66/2017, art. 8). Each school prepares it
  within the three-year plan of the educational offer: it defines how resources are used and how barriers are overcome, in
  order to design and plan the actions that improve the quality of inclusion. The article does not name the library:
  whether and how the library appears in it is for the school to decide.

None of these rules sets functional requirements for library software. The part that concerns BiblioViva directly, as a
work tool of the staff, is the accessibility of the interface, covered in the page [Accessibility](accessibility.md).

## What BiblioViva does

### For whoever works in the library

The staff interface is designed to meet WCAG 2.2 level AA: use with the keyboard alone, screen readers, magnification,
colour contrast. The criteria, the checks and the known limits are in the page [Accessibility](accessibility.md).

### For the students

BiblioViva has no public catalogue and no accounts for students: the student does not use the software, the staff use it
at the desk. It contains no diagnoses, personalised learning plans or health data of the students, and it has no function
to mark that a student has a DSA or a disability.

To help whoever chooses a book for a student, the catalogue offers:

- The materials. The **Materials** list of the **Library and school** section of the Configuration holds the library's
  types of material (book, DVD, magazine…). The administrator adds others, for example a material for audiobooks, and the
  **Material** filter of the catalogue makes it possible to search for that material only. Each material can have its own
  loan rules, such as the duration and the number of books (see [Set the loan rules](../admin/guides/set-loan-rules.md)).
- The reading levels. If the library uses them, fiction carries a **Level**, a code of a scale that the school defines in
  the **Reading levels** panel of the classification; the codes supplied with BiblioViva go from 1 to 5. The **Level**
  filter of the catalogue finds the fiction of one level. The scale is the school's: BiblioViva does not say to which
  reading difficulty a level corresponds, nor does it link it to a DSA.
- The subjects. The **Subjects** field of a book's record holds keywords chosen by the school, and the catalogue search
  finds them.
- The **Physical description** and the book's notes, free-text fields.

BiblioViva has no field dedicated to the accessible format of a book: high legibility, large print, symbols for
augmentative and alternative communication, digital format for speech synthesis. The school can express it with a
material of its own, with a subject adopted by everyone (for example "high legibility") or with the **Physical
description**. Of these, only the material has a catalogue filter and loan rules of its own; the subject is found with the
search; the physical description is a text to read on the record.

BiblioViva does not keep the files of digital books and does not read them aloud: it records the physical copy or the
medium (for example a CD) and its loan.

## What the school does

- Decide with the inclusion officers whether the library is among the tools provided by the inclusion plan and by the
  personalised learning plans, and which accessible materials to acquire.
- Choose how to distinguish the accessible materials in the catalogue: a dedicated material, a subject, a physical
  description; then always use the same way.
- Provide the staff with disabilities with the assistive technology they need at the desk, as said in the page
  [Accessibility](accessibility.md).
- Do not record in BiblioViva diagnoses or health conditions of the students, not even in the notes: they are special
  categories of data (art. 9 of the GDPR), for which the school has other tools. See [Personal data
  protection](data-protection.md).
